# Memory Seed Marketing Constitution

## Status and purpose

This is the living doctrine for how Memory Seed earns attention, builds understanding, and converts
conviction into action. It governs marketing strategy, campaigns, content, and calls to action.

It begins as a draft. Ratification means agreeing to use it as the standard against which marketing
choices are made. Amendments should preserve the intent of the framework while improving how it is
applied.

## The governing framework

> Entertain the mass, educate the interested, and sell to the committed.

Marketing is a progression of audience readiness, not one message forced onto everyone:

1. **Entertain the mass** — earn attention from people who may not know Memory Seed or feel the
   problem yet.
2. **Educate the interested** — help people who recognise the problem understand it, its cost, and
   the available approach.
3. **Sell to the committed** — give people who believe in the problem and approach a clear, credible,
   low-friction next step.

Every marketing asset must have one primary stage. It may support an adjacent stage, but it should
not try to perform all three jobs at once.

## Article I — Entertain the mass

### Audience

People with no prior knowledge of Memory Seed and little or no active buying intent.

### Job

Create relevance, recognition, curiosity, or emotional resonance around the experience of losing
project context and repeating old reasoning.

### We will

- Lead with a human truth, tension, story, surprise, demonstration, or useful observation.
- Make the idea understandable without requiring product knowledge or specialist vocabulary.
- Respect the audience's time: the content must be worthwhile even if they never become a customer.
- Give interested people a natural path to learn more.

### We will not

- Disguise a sales pitch as entertainment.
- Manufacture fear, outrage, false urgency, or artificial controversy.
- Optimise reach at the expense of relevance to the problem Memory Seed solves.
- Measure success by impressions alone.

### Typical formats

Short videos, visual demonstrations, stories, memes, observations, founder perspectives, relatable
failure moments, and opinion-led posts.

### Primary evidence of success

Qualified attention: completion, sharing, meaningful engagement, profile or site visits, and movement
into educational content.

## Article II — Educate the interested

### Audience

People who recognise the problem and want to understand its causes, consequences, or possible
solutions.

### Job

Turn curiosity into informed conviction. Teach the category and approach clearly enough that the
audience can judge whether Memory Seed is relevant to them.

### We will

- Teach the problem before insisting on the product.
- Explain decisions, evidence, trade-offs, and limits in plain language.
- Separate demonstrated product behaviour, sourced external facts, and our own hypotheses.
- Use concrete examples that connect project-memory failure to real work.
- Help people self-qualify, including recognising when Memory Seed is not for them.
- Offer a clear next step for those ready to evaluate or adopt.

### We will not

- Use complexity to create artificial authority.
- Make claims that outrun the evidence.
- Hide material limitations or imply that Memory Seed replaces adjacent tools it is designed to
  complement.
- Gate every useful explanation behind an email form.

### Typical formats

Guides, explainers, case studies, comparisons, technical walkthroughs, founder essays, webinars,
documentation-led content, and evidence-backed research.

### Primary evidence of success

Qualified learning: saves, return visits, deeper content consumption, newsletter or product-interest
sign-ups, evaluation starts, and informed questions.

## Article III — Sell to the committed

### Audience

People who understand the problem, accept the approach, and are deciding whether to act now, act
later, or choose another solution.

### Job

Make the value, offer, proof, risk, and next action unmistakably clear.

### We will

- State what is offered, for whom, at what cost, and what happens next.
- Match proof to the promise: demonstrations, customer evidence, product behaviour, or transparent
  founder evidence as appropriate.
- Address real objections and switching costs directly.
- Reduce friction without creating pressure.
- Make the call to action specific, proportionate, and easy to complete.

### We will not

- Create false scarcity, fake deadlines, or manipulative urgency.
- Treat attention or curiosity as purchase intent.
- Hide price, commitment, material conditions, or the limits of an early product.
- Promise outcomes that Memory Seed cannot control.

### Typical formats

Product pages, pricing pages, demos, onboarding sequences, evaluation guides, comparison pages,
offers, sales conversations, and direct calls to action.

### Primary evidence of success

Committed action: installation, activation, trial or evaluation completion, purchase, retained use,
referral, or another explicitly chosen conversion.

## Article IV — The handoff between stages

The framework works only when each stage hands the right people to the next stage.

- Entertainment should point to a relevant explanation, not jump immediately to a hard sale.
- Education should point to an evaluation or offer when the audience has enough context to decide.
- Sales should answer the remaining decision questions rather than restart broad awareness messaging.
- A person may enter at any stage. We respond to demonstrated readiness, not an assumed linear funnel.
- One strong idea should be adapted across stages, but the message and call to action must change with
  audience intent.

## Article V — Voice and character

Memory Seed marketing should be:

- **Clear:** plain language before category jargon.
- **Intelligent:** thoughtful without performing cleverness.
- **Grounded:** evidence and working demonstrations before grand claims.
- **Human:** concerned with the lived cost of forgotten context, not just system architecture.
- **Calmly confident:** specific about value, candid about uncertainty, and free of hype.
- **Respectful:** useful before extractive, and persuasive without coercion.

## Article VI — Truth, trust, and ethical limits

- Users own their project memory; marketing must never imply that Memory Seed needs to own their data
  to deliver its core value.
- The local-first, model-independent core and its limitations must be represented accurately.
- Product claims must be traceable to current behaviour or clearly labelled as direction, hypothesis,
  or planned capability.
- Customer stories require permission and must not expose confidential project context.
- Competitor comparisons must be current, fair, and based on like-for-like evidence.
- Marketing must preserve trust even when a more aggressive tactic could improve a short-term metric.

## Article VII — Decision test

Before publishing or funding a marketing asset, answer:

1. Which stage is primary: entertain, educate, or sell?
2. Who is the audience, and what evidence shows they are at that stage?
3. What single job should this asset do?
4. Is it valuable and truthful if the audience never buys?
5. What is the natural next step, and is it proportionate to their readiness?
6. What evidence would show that the asset moved the right people forward?
7. Does any claim conflict with current product behaviour or the Memory Seed Constitution?

If the stage, audience, job, or next step cannot be stated plainly, the asset is not ready.

## Article IX — Governance and amendment

This document is a living commercial doctrine, not product law. It is subordinate to the ratified
Memory Seed Constitution wherever product truth, user ownership, trust, or system invariants are
concerned.

Changes fall into two classes:

- **Refinement:** examples, formats, measures, or wording that preserve the three-stage framework.
- **Amendment:** a change to the framework, ethical limits, voice, or rules for how stages interact.

Record meaningful amendments below so the marketing doctrine can evolve without silently changing
its principles.
