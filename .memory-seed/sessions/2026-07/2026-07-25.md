### Summary

- Made `_chunk_from_storage` tolerant of a cache written by an older binary, so a stale projection degrades to a rebuild instead of 500-ing the whole Trail.

### Decision

- D: The cached-chunk loader no longer trusts the stored shape blindly. A renamed field is canonicalised (`supersedes` → `replaces`, merged without duplicates) so its edges survive; any other unknown key is dropped rather than passed to `MemoryChunk(**data)`. A stale cache therefore degrades to a rebuild, never a hard crash.
- R: A pre-rename cache carrying `supersedes` raised `unexpected keyword argument` and took the entire Trail down (500 on every request) until the cache file was found and cleared by hand — observed live 2026-07-24 when an old orphaned server's cache was read by the new binary. The cache is a rebuildable projection (Constitution: every DB/cache is derived), so a crash on READ is strictly worse than a stale read: the stale read loses at most one field until the next rebuild, the crash loses the UI. Canonicalising the renamed field also honours the vocabulary rename's standing promise to read the old spelling forever, which the cache reader was silently violating.
- A: Bump a cache schema version so a shape change invalidates the cache proactively — the more thorough fix, but bigger, and it does not help a cache already on disk from a mismatched binary; deferred. Just deleting the cache on mismatch — rejected as more destructive than needed when the data is readable after canonicalisation.
- F: `memory-trace/memory_trace/service.py` (`_chunk_from_storage`, `fields` import).
- T: `memory-trace/tests/test_incremental_projection.py::ChunkStorageSchemaDriftTests` — reverting the fix reproduces the exact live `TypeError: unexpected keyword argument 'supersedes'`; with it, the renamed field canonicalises, merges without duplicates, and an unknown key is dropped. 207 memory-trace tests pass.

### Summary

- Assessed JNL's three topic-vocabulary ideas against corpus data: raised the inferred-topic cap to 4, wrote a proposal deferring decision-level topic keying, and rejected a third axis.

### Decisions

#### D1 - Raise the inferred-topic cap to 4 to match the corpus, not hold the machine stricter than the author

- D: `MAX_INFERRED_TOPICS` goes from 3 to 4 — the corpus authored maximum. The topic-sidecar overreach check fires at 5+.
- R: JNL's intuition that multi-decision entries touch more topics is confirmed by measurement: 2- and 3+-decision entries average 2.76 / 2.60 topics vs 2.10 for single-decision, and authored entries reach 4. The sidecar cap was 3, so an inferred fourth topic on a genuinely four-facet entry would be rejected — the inference path held to a stricter standard than the author holds themselves, which is backwards. The cap is kept (not removed): a label applied to everything distinguishes nothing, and the effect is bounded — nothing in the corpus exceeds 4.
- A: A decision-aware cap (`min(4, 2 + decisions)`) — rejected as more machinery for a ~0.6-topic difference; a flat 4 matching the corpus max is simpler and correct. Removing the cap entirely — rejected: the restraint guard is the reason it exists.
- F: `memory_seed/core.py` (`MAX_INFERRED_TOPICS`), `tests/test_links_check.py` (cap test now asserts 4 passes, 5 is overreach; test vocab widened), `docs/3_Spec/graph-edge-contract.md`.
- T: 922 backend tests pass; corpus links check clean.

#### D2 - Defer decision-level topic keying to a proposal, and reject a third axis

- D: Keying each topic to its decision (`topic:dN`) is written up as a proposal and deferred, not built. A third mandatory topic axis is rejected. The proposal doc also records the live topic-inference conventions (two-axis model, cross-cutting concerns as rare add-ons, the cap) — the durable home for what the backfill swarm prompt teaches.
- R: `topic:dN` would mirror the `:dN` lifecycle grammar and unify the multi-decision question elegantly, but topics are a fuzzy associative signal where per-decision precision fights their purpose (a lifecycle edge is a precise claim; a topic is soft aboutness). The swarm already struggles with the coarse version (28% exact on the blind pilot), so per-decision attribution would make the 206-entry backfill less reliable, at full-stack cost (grammar, parser, chunk shape, every consumer) for a thin payoff. A third axis has no home in the vocabulary beyond three low-use cross-cutting slugs (encoding/perf/security, ~11 uses total) and would inflate topics/entry past the measured 2.26 norm. The `:dN` precedent makes decision-level topics cheap to add later if a concrete consumer needs it (e.g. tinting Trail decision rows by topic).
- F: `docs/2_Todo/decision-level-topics-proposal.md` (new).
- T: `docs check` clean on the new doc; `docs index` regenerated.
