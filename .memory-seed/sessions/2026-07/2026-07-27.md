### Summary

- Closed the seed/live parity gap flagged as deferred on 2026-07-26: every skill runbook shipped by
  `SEED_FILES` is now asserted byte-identical to its live twin, instead of only the nine skills
  hand-listed in the `extracted` dict plus three explicit pairs.
- All 26 twins were already byte-identical at the time of writing, `link_swarm.md` included — the
  defect was the absence of enforcement, not drift. 768 tests pass (was 767).

### Decisions

#### D1 - One generic loop over SEED_FILES, comparing `seed_file.source` bytes

- D: `test_every_seeded_skill_runbook_matches_its_live_twin` iterates `SEED_FILES`, keeps the entries
  whose destination starts with `.memory-seed/skills/`, and asserts
  `Path(destination).read_bytes() == seed_file.source.read_bytes()` — 25 runbooks today. The
  hand-listed per-skill `assertEqual`s are deliberately left in place.
- R: The three existing SEED_FILES tests enforce **presence** and **registration** — a skill exists in
  both trees and something triggers it — and none of them reads content, so a skill outside the
  hand-lists could drift silently. Coverage that depends on an author remembering to extend a dict is
  the failure mode itself: `link_swarm.md` shipped through that gap. The redundancy with the
  hand-listed skills is intentional and worth its cost, because the two tests assert different things
  — those pin per-skill CONTENT anchors, this pins COVERAGE — and a future reader who mistakes them
  for duplicates would delete the wrong one.
- A: Re-deriving the seed path as `SEED_SKILLS_DIR / name` (the shape of the existing
  `_seed_files_skill_names()` helper) was rejected: it discards the source and the two paths are
  identical today, so the test would pass either way and never catch a `SeedFile` wired to the wrong
  source. `read_text()` was rejected for a measured reason, not a stylistic one — rewriting
  `link_swarm.md`'s seed twin to CRLF leaves `read_text()` comparing **equal** while `read_bytes()`
  fails, and a Windows/OneDrive checkout is exactly where that divergence appears.
- F: `tests/test_session_schema.py`.
- T: Full suite 768 passed, 17 subtests, 337s. Two negative controls, both restored via
  `git checkout --`: appending bytes to the `link_swarm.md` seed twin fails the test naming
  `link_swarm.md`; the CRLF rewrite fails it while `read_text()` compares equal. A floor assertion
  (`len(checked) >= 25`, and `skipped == [index.md]`) guards the vacuous pass, since a filtered-loop
  test that matches nothing is green and silent.

#### D2 - `skills/index.md` is the sole exemption; `.agents/` is excluded structurally, not by rule

- D: `.memory-seed/skills/index.md` is skipped and the skip is asserted to be the only one. `.agents/`
  persona templates are not mentioned in the filter at all — they simply never match the
  `.memory-seed/skills/` prefix.
- R: `index.md` is runtime-local: projects extend the trigger registry with persona entries, so exact
  equality is the wrong assertion, and `test_skill_trigger_registry_is_deterministic_and_seeded`
  already checks seed content for per-line containment. Asserting the skip-list rather than just
  skipping means a second exemption cannot be added quietly. For `.agents/`, the measurement settles
  it: 4 of the 7 live personas (`content-creator`, `copywriter`, `developer`, `solo-founder`) already
  differ from their seed sources, so a parity assertion over them would fail on contact. That
  divergence is the design working — personas are project-local and evolve with user approval, which
  is why `test_control_plane_files_report_current_version` already skips them — not a defect to chase.
  Excluding them structurally rather than by an explicit carve-out keeps the test's scope legible:
  it is about shipped skill runbooks, and nothing else was ever in range.
- F: `tests/test_session_schema.py`.
- T: `cmp` across all 7 `.agents/` twins and all 26 skill twins recorded the counts above.
