### Summary

- Five defects JNL found by looking at the shipped decision-row Graph, plus the measured cause of

### Decisions

#### D1 - `linked` scope selects entries, not ordinals

- D: `decision_row_scope: "linked"` now selects ENTRIES and expands each selected one WHOLE.
  `_expand_decision_rows` takes `only_entries` in place of `only_ordinals`.
- R: JNL reported groups showing "1 less decision than is present". It was not an off-by-one — it was
  per-ordinal selection: `mse_a0bxp5n1wcnsjxvw` has 4 decisions and drew d1/d3/d4,
  `mse_m0xs623m4cs0kjag` has 2 and drew only d2. The gaps land wherever no edge names an ordinal.
  A reader cannot tell a decision with **no relationships** from a decision that **does not exist**, so
  a container that claims to hold an entry has to hold all of it — otherwise the group misrepresents
  the memory it frames. Per-ordinal filtering was only ever defensible because an edgeless row arrived
  as a fresh isolate in a force layout (measured: 276 of 446); compound containment retired that
  reasoning on 2026-07-26, so the constraint could go. Selection is still by evidence — an entry that
  no decision edge names is not expanded at all — so `linked` remains a narrowing, not `all`.
- A: Prune the edgeless rows instead (a task chip was already running for exactly this) — rejected and
  the session stopped: it optimises the wrong direction, since the rows are the thing missing.
- F: `memory-trace/memory_trace/service.py`, `memory-trace/tests/test_trail_decision_edges.py`.

#### D2 - A decision row's visibility and credit both follow its ANCHOR

- D: One rule, `visibilityIdFor`, applied in two places: the Orphans filter keeps or drops a row with
  its anchor, and the Inspector's **Links** count reads endpoints through it.
- R: D1 alone made things briefly worse: the completed rows carried no edge, failed the connectedness
  test on their own degree, and were dropped — so with Orphans off a 4-decision entry drew a ring of
  **one**. And separately JNL found `mse_5zg50mzrmtx80c80` reporting "Links 0" while its d2 row carried
  two lines, because the count compared raw endpoint ids. Both are the same mistake: reading a decision
  row as if it were an entry. A decision inside a shown entry is not an orphan, it is a decision; and a
  line terminating on a row is a line that entry has. The Links case is the worse misreading of the
  two — those entries are precisely the ones decision rows exist to serve, and the panel was telling
  the reader their work was unconnected.
- A: Credit rows as edge endpoints in `connectedIdsWithDecisionAnchors` — rejected: that set answers
  "what did an author link", and inventing membership in it would leak a derived fact into an authored
  channel. Mapping at the point of USE keeps the derivation where it belongs.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphDecisionRows.ts`, `GraphWorkspace.tsx`, `App.tsx`.

#### D3 - The circle is a halo child, because a compound parent cannot be one

- D: The group's compound parent is now invisible and carries only containment; an ordinary child node
  (`dhalo:{entry_id}`), sized by `haloDiameter` and centred on the anchor, draws the visible circle.
- R: JNL asked for circles rather than boxes, which is right — the rows sit on a ring, so a rectangle
  drew corners the content never reaches and read as a container of records rather than one memory with
  its decisions. Setting `shape: ellipse` on the parent did nothing: `style()` reported `ellipse` and
  the screen showed a square, because Cytoscape renders a compound parent as a rectangle regardless.
  Splitting the two jobs keeps the structural channel intact (the `parent` field is still what
  expresses containment) and puts the drawing on an element that can actually be a circle.
- A: Drop the compound parent and keep only the halo — rejected: containment would stop being
  structural, which is the whole basis of the 2026-07-26 non-edge decision.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphDecisionRows.ts`, `GraphWorkspace.tsx`.

#### D4 - The ring is rigid but free to rotate

- D: Rows sit at equal angles (`360/N`, hard constraint) on a ring whose PHASE is fitted in closed form
  to the group's own decision edges — `ringPhase`, the circular mean of each row's target direction less
  the slot it occupies. Undetermined fits rest at 12 o'clock.
- R: Two of JNL's instructions in sequence: equal angles first, then rotational freedom "while
  preserving the equidistant angle between each decision too". They are only in tension if each row is
  aimed independently — which is what the original code did, and it bought the direction hint by
  spending the spacing. A rigid ring with one degree of freedom keeps both: spacing cannot vary, and the
  single free parameter is exactly enough to put a row on the side its relative is on. The closed form
  matters for determinism: no search, one pass, same answer every load. The fallback matters for
  stability: two rows pulling opposite ways leave the rotation genuinely undetermined, and picking one
  of two equally good answers would make the ring flip between them every frame.
- A: Rotate to avoid the anchor's own entry-level edges instead of toward counterparts — rejected: it
  optimises against lines that are not the group's, and the group's own edges are the information a
  reader is trying to follow.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphDecisionRows.ts`, `GraphWorkspace.tsx`.

### Summary

- Closed the Show-more freeze, after measuring JNL's zonal-rendering hypothesis and finding it does
  not hold on this renderer.

### Decisions

#### D1 - Zonal settling is declined on measurement, not on taste

- D: No centre-first, ripple-outward settle. The physics keeps running over the whole graph at once.
- R: JNL asked whether focusing compute on the centre and rippling out would help. Measured at ~1180
  elements: nothing moving is a 16.6ms frame, **one** node moving is **81.9ms**, and all 303 moving is
  93.6ms. So a single moved node already costs 87% of moving everything — zoning the physics saves ~12%,
  and staging the settle means MORE frames in which something moved, so the total gets worse rather
  than better. Two follow-ups closed the neighbouring ideas: drawing fewer elements is only weakly
  cheaper (hiding 47% of them moved the frame 94.7ms → 76.4ms), and distance moved does not matter at
  all (0.5px 147.8ms vs 1.5px 146.1ms in the same machine state). The cost is the act of repainting,
  not the movement in it — which is a property of the renderer, and the reason the intuition fails.
- A: Progressive DISCLOSURE (hold the periphery back, reveal outward) — declined for now on the same
  evidence: it is the strongest form of "draw less", and draw-less measured at 19% for halving the
  canvas. Recorded rather than dropped, because it is the right lever if element counts grow much
  further.
- F: none — this decision is what was NOT built.

#### D2 - Several physics steps per painted frame, scaled by element count

- D: `ticksPerPaint(elements)` runs 1 step per painted frame below ~400 elements and up to 3 above, and
  the settle loop ticks that many times before each paint.
- R: The only quantity that scales with the cost is the number of PAINTS, so paint less often. Alpha
  decays per tick, so N ticks per frame reaches rest after the same number of ticks in 1/N the frames:
  the settle comes out cheaper **and shorter**, which is the opposite of the usual perf trade. Scaled
  rather than flat because a small graph already paints inside its frame budget and has nothing to buy;
  capped at 3 so the coarsest case still reads as movement rather than teleporting.
- A: Lower the starting alpha further — already falsified earlier tonight: ticks scale with
  `log(alpha0/alphaMin)`, so alpha 0.4 buys 14%. Converge off-screen in one block above a size
  threshold — declined by JNL, who chose to keep the animation.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphForces.ts`, `graphForces.test.ts`,
  `memory-trace/client/src/GraphWorkspace.tsx`.
- T: 168 client unit tests (the `ticksPerPaint` boundaries and its degenerate inputs included), 7 story
  tests, typecheck clean, build OK. Verified live by counting Cytoscape `render` events for one settle
  at 1463 elements: **47 renders, 48 long tasks** — one long task per painted frame, and ~141 ticks
  delivered in 47 frames at `ticksPerPaint` 3, where before it was one render per tick.

### Follow-up

- The wall-clock number is currently **loaded and should not be quoted as a baseline**: 9.5s blocked of
  14.2s, at 198ms per painted frame, while this machine ran four concurrent agent sessions. The same
  probe read 147ms where it read 93ms an hour earlier, so the machine is ~1.6-2x slower than when the
  original 74ms/frame figure was taken. Unloaded, 47 frames x ~85ms is ~4s against ~11s before. Re-measure
  on an idle machine before treating any absolute figure here as the new baseline.
- Absolute timings across a long session are unreliable for exactly that reason. What survived the noise
  was every RATIO measured inside a single run — 1 node vs 303, hidden vs drawn, 0.5px vs 1.5px, and the
  render count per settle. Prefer within-run ratios when measuring this on a shared machine.
