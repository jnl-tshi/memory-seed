### Summary

- Five defects JNL found by looking at the shipped decision-row Graph, plus the measured cause of
  the "Show more locks scrolling for a minute" report.

### Decisions

#### D1 - `linked` scope selects entries, not ordinals

- D: `decision_row_scope: "linked"` now selects ENTRIES and expands each selected one WHOLE.
  `_expand_decision_rows` takes `only_entries` in place of `only_ordinals`.
- R: JNL reported groups showing "1 less decision than is present". It was not an off-by-one — it was
  per-ordinal selection: `mse_a0bxp5n1wcnsjxvw` has 4 decisions and drew d1/d3/d4,
  `mse_m0xs623m4cs0kjag` has 2 and drew only d2. The gaps land wherever no edge names an ordinal.
  A reader cannot tell a decision with **no relationships** from a decision that **does not exist**, so
  a container that claims to hold an entry has to hold all of it — otherwise the group misrepresents
  the memory it frames. Per-ordinal filtering was only ever defensible because an edgeless row arrived
  as a fresh isolate in a force layout (measured: 276 of 446); compound containment retired that
  reasoning on 2026-07-26, so the constraint could go. Selection is still by evidence — an entry that
  no decision edge names is not expanded at all — so `linked` remains a narrowing, not `all`.
- A: Prune the edgeless rows instead (a task chip was already running for exactly this) — rejected and
  the session stopped: it optimises the wrong direction, since the rows are the thing missing.
- F: `memory-trace/memory_trace/service.py`, `memory-trace/tests/test_trail_decision_edges.py`.

#### D2 - A decision row's visibility and credit both follow its ANCHOR

- D: One rule, `visibilityIdFor`, applied in two places: the Orphans filter keeps or drops a row with
  its anchor, and the Inspector's **Links** count reads endpoints through it.
- R: D1 alone made things briefly worse: the completed rows carried no edge, failed the connectedness
  test on their own degree, and were dropped — so with Orphans off a 4-decision entry drew a ring of
  **one**. And separately JNL found `mse_5zg50mzrmtx80c80` reporting "Links 0" while its d2 row carried
  two lines, because the count compared raw endpoint ids. Both are the same mistake: reading a decision
  row as if it were an entry. A decision inside a shown entry is not an orphan, it is a decision; and a
  line terminating on a row is a line that entry has. The Links case is the worse misreading of the
  two — those entries are precisely the ones decision rows exist to serve, and the panel was telling
  the reader their work was unconnected.
- A: Credit rows as edge endpoints in `connectedIdsWithDecisionAnchors` — rejected: that set answers
  "what did an author link", and inventing membership in it would leak a derived fact into an authored
  channel. Mapping at the point of USE keeps the derivation where it belongs.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphDecisionRows.ts`, `GraphWorkspace.tsx`, `App.tsx`.

#### D3 - The circle is a halo child, because a compound parent cannot be one

- D: The group's compound parent is now invisible and carries only containment; an ordinary child node
  (`dhalo:{entry_id}`), sized by `haloDiameter` and centred on the anchor, draws the visible circle.
- R: JNL asked for circles rather than boxes, which is right — the rows sit on a ring, so a rectangle
  drew corners the content never reaches and read as a container of records rather than one memory with
  its decisions. Setting `shape: ellipse` on the parent did nothing: `style()` reported `ellipse` and
  the screen showed a square, because Cytoscape renders a compound parent as a rectangle regardless.
  Splitting the two jobs keeps the structural channel intact (the `parent` field is still what
  expresses containment) and puts the drawing on an element that can actually be a circle.
- A: Drop the compound parent and keep only the halo — rejected: containment would stop being
  structural, which is the whole basis of the 2026-07-26 non-edge decision.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphDecisionRows.ts`, `GraphWorkspace.tsx`.

#### D4 - The ring is rigid but free to rotate

- D: Rows sit at equal angles (`360/N`, hard constraint) on a ring whose PHASE is fitted in closed form
  to the group's own decision edges — `ringPhase`, the circular mean of each row's target direction less
  the slot it occupies. Undetermined fits rest at 12 o'clock.
- R: Two of JNL's instructions in sequence: equal angles first, then rotational freedom "while
  preserving the equidistant angle between each decision too". They are only in tension if each row is
  aimed independently — which is what the original code did, and it bought the direction hint by
  spending the spacing. A rigid ring with one degree of freedom keeps both: spacing cannot vary, and the
  single free parameter is exactly enough to put a row on the side its relative is on. The closed form
  matters for determinism: no search, one pass, same answer every load. The fallback matters for
  stability: two rows pulling opposite ways leave the rotation genuinely undetermined, and picking one
  of two equally good answers would make the ring flip between them every frame.
- A: Rotate to avoid the anchor's own entry-level edges instead of toward counterparts — rejected: it
  optimises against lines that are not the group's, and the group's own edges are the information a
  reader is trying to follow.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphDecisionRows.ts`, `GraphWorkspace.tsx`.

#### D5 - The settle re-measures the camera 4x a second, and a warm mount stops early

- D: `scaleToFitGrowth` is throttled to 250ms instead of every tick, and a warm-seeded mount runs from
  alpha 0.6 to an alpha floor of 0.03 instead of 1.0 to 0.001.
- R: JNL reported scrolling locked for about a minute after "Show more", cause unclear. Measured: idle
  is clean (**0** long tasks in 10.8s, worst frame 18ms), and the whole cost is the settle — ~365 long
  tasks averaging **74ms**, **27 of 37 seconds** with the main thread blocked. So nothing can scroll,
  and the fetches are not to blame (176–532ms each).
  Two cheap and invisible wins. `cy.elements().boundingBox()` ran **every tick** and costs 8.65ms at 294
  nodes / 13.48ms at 1463 elements — more than the physics and the paint together — to answer a question
  whose answer changes far slower than 60Hz. And the number of TICKS is what sets the duration: ~300 from
  alpha 1 to the d3 default floor. A warm mount does not need that budget, since every incumbent came
  back from a settled layout and only the arriving page has to travel; stopping at 0.03 gives ~130.
  Lowering the START alpha alone would have bought 14%, because ticks scale with `log(alpha0/alphaMin)`
  — worth recording, because it is the intuitive lever and it is the wrong one.
- A: Converge off-screen above a node threshold (instant, no motion) — offered and declined by JNL, who
  chose to keep the animation.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/GraphWorkspace.tsx`.
- T: 980 backend tests, 167 client unit tests, 7 story tests, typecheck clean, build OK. Verified live
  against the real corpus: group ordinals match the authored `#### Dn` count exactly on every sampled
  entry (4/4, 3/3, 3/3, 2/2, 2/2); 12 groups drawn with **12 distinct rotations** and spacing exactly
  `360/N` in every one; halo `shape` is `ellipse` and encloses the ring at every row count;
  `mse_5zg50mzrmtx80c80` now reads Links **2**.

### Follow-up

- **The freeze is not fixed, and D5 alone cannot fix it.** With the app's own per-frame work down to
  ~4.5ms, the measured frame is still 150–320ms at 1463 elements, so ~97% of it is Cytoscape's canvas
  redraw — and D1 legitimately grew the element count (169 rows where there were 92, plus 67 halos),
  which ate the saving at large slices. One Show more at 689 nodes still blocks ~13–19s. The remaining
  lever is the one JNL declined while it was only a nicety: paint every 2nd or 3rd tick, which cuts
  redraws proportionally and makes the settle SHORTER rather than choppier (alpha decays per tick, so
  the same ~130 ticks land in ~45 frames). Worth re-offering now that it is the deciding factor rather
  than an optimisation.
- An earlier measurement in this session was inflated 3x: arming a `PerformanceObserver` repeatedly
  without disconnecting the previous ones delivers every entry once per observer. The "80.8 seconds of
  blocking in 37 seconds" that first looked alarming was three copies of ~27s. Deduplicating by
  `(startTime, duration)` is what caught it.

## 2026-07-27 01:27 - Zonal settling measured and declined; the settle repaints less often instead

### Summary

- Closed the Show-more freeze, after measuring JNL's zonal-rendering hypothesis and finding it does
  not hold on this renderer.

### Decisions

#### D1 - Zonal settling is declined on measurement, not on taste

- D: No centre-first, ripple-outward settle. The physics keeps running over the whole graph at once.
- R: JNL asked whether focusing compute on the centre and rippling out would help. Measured at ~1180
  elements: nothing moving is a 16.6ms frame, **one** node moving is **81.9ms**, and all 303 moving is
  93.6ms. So a single moved node already costs 87% of moving everything — zoning the physics saves ~12%,
  and staging the settle means MORE frames in which something moved, so the total gets worse rather
  than better. Two follow-ups closed the neighbouring ideas: drawing fewer elements is only weakly
  cheaper (hiding 47% of them moved the frame 94.7ms → 76.4ms), and distance moved does not matter at
  all (0.5px 147.8ms vs 1.5px 146.1ms in the same machine state). The cost is the act of repainting,
  not the movement in it — which is a property of the renderer, and the reason the intuition fails.
- A: Progressive DISCLOSURE (hold the periphery back, reveal outward) — declined for now on the same
  evidence: it is the strongest form of "draw less", and draw-less measured at 19% for halving the
  canvas. Recorded rather than dropped, because it is the right lever if element counts grow much
  further.
- F: none — this decision is what was NOT built.

#### D2 - Several physics steps per painted frame, scaled by element count

- D: `ticksPerPaint(elements)` runs 1 step per painted frame below ~400 elements and up to 3 above, and
  the settle loop ticks that many times before each paint.
- R: The only quantity that scales with the cost is the number of PAINTS, so paint less often. Alpha
  decays per tick, so N ticks per frame reaches rest after the same number of ticks in 1/N the frames:
  the settle comes out cheaper **and shorter**, which is the opposite of the usual perf trade. Scaled
  rather than flat because a small graph already paints inside its frame budget and has nothing to buy;
  capped at 3 so the coarsest case still reads as movement rather than teleporting.
- A: Lower the starting alpha further — already falsified earlier tonight: ticks scale with
  `log(alpha0/alphaMin)`, so alpha 0.4 buys 14%. Converge off-screen in one block above a size
  threshold — declined by JNL, who chose to keep the animation.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphForces.ts`, `graphForces.test.ts`,
  `memory-trace/client/src/GraphWorkspace.tsx`.
- T: 168 client unit tests (the `ticksPerPaint` boundaries and its degenerate inputs included), 7 story
  tests, typecheck clean, build OK. Verified live by counting Cytoscape `render` events for one settle
  at 1463 elements: **47 renders, 48 long tasks** — one long task per painted frame, and ~141 ticks
  delivered in 47 frames at `ticksPerPaint` 3, where before it was one render per tick.

### Follow-up

- The wall-clock number is currently **loaded and should not be quoted as a baseline**: 9.5s blocked of
  14.2s, at 198ms per painted frame, while this machine ran four concurrent agent sessions. The same
  probe read 147ms where it read 93ms an hour earlier, so the machine is ~1.6-2x slower than when the
  original 74ms/frame figure was taken. Unloaded, 47 frames x ~85ms is ~4s against ~11s before. Re-measure
  on an idle machine before treating any absolute figure here as the new baseline.
- Absolute timings across a long session are unreliable for exactly that reason. What survived the noise
  was every RATIO measured inside a single run — 1 node vs 303, hidden vs drawn, 0.5px vs 1.5px, and the
  render count per settle. Prefer within-run ratios when measuring this on a shared machine.

### Summary

- The graph now colours by topic ROOT with nothing filtered and by CHILD inside a focused family,
  which needed a new facet to keep an alias from posing as a child.

### Decisions

#### D1 - The colour key is the root, except inside the focus where it is the child

- D: `colourKeyOf(slug, roots, focus, canonical)`. With no filter the key is the root, as before.
  Filtered to a family, slugs inside that family key on their own canonical slug; everything outside
  keeps its root.
- R: Root colour is what makes the whole map splittable at a glance — and applied to a filtered view it
  paints everything one colour, because every node now shares the family and the family has stopped
  being information. JNL asked for the reversal, and the corpus can already show it: 43 child slugs are
  authored today (`goal` 6, `merge` 5, `branch-history` 3), so focusing `git-workflow` separates real
  entries rather than waiting on the sweep. Outside the focus the root still reads, because a node can
  carry a second topic from another family and that family is still what identifies it.
- A: Colour by child always — rejected, and it is the decision the root-colouring work already made:
  the palette would grow and reshuffle as a sweep moves entries onto children. The point is that root
  colour is right for the corpus view and wrong for the family view, not that either is right always.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphCommunities.ts`, `GraphWorkspace.tsx`, `App.tsx`.

#### D2 - A colour is only spent when it separates something

- D: A focused family whose members reduce to ONE canonical slug mints no key at all; the member falls
  back to its parent's colour. Two or more and each earns its own.
- R: JNL: "if only a child is present derive its parent colour". A lone member has nothing to
  distinguish, so a new hue would be a new category the reader has to learn for no gain — and it would
  cost the recognisable family colour they already know. Implemented as a MISSED LOOKUP rather than a
  branch: `topicColourScale` tries the focused key and falls back to the root, so "derive the parent"
  is the absence of a slot rather than a rule that could disagree with the slot table.
- A: Decide it from what is on screen rather than from the corpus — rejected: colour would then shift
  as the view pages, which is the property the corpus-wide counts exist to protect.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphCommunities.ts`.

#### D3 - Focused keys are APPENDED past the root table, never interleaved

- D: The root slot table is built exactly as before; the focused family's members are appended after it.
- R: The first version expanded into the ordered table, and its own test caught the consequence
  immediately: `memory-trace` moved from `#0a9a94` to `#43bdd4` merely because `git-workflow` was
  focused. That is precisely the instability that put colour on the root in the first place — an
  ordered table means an inserted key shifts every slot after it and repaints communities that did not
  change. Appending makes focusing a strictly additive operation.
- A: Sort the whole table with children included — rejected on the same measurement; it is the
  interleaving that does the damage, not the ordering rule.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphCommunities.ts`, `graphCommunities.test.ts`.

#### D4 - A new facet, because roots cannot tell an alias from a child

- D: `Facets.topic_canonical` (every authorable name -> the canonical slug it denotes) joins
  `topic_roots`. Read only where colour is keyed below the root.
- R: Both an alias and a child map to a root, so through `topic_roots` alone `perf`/`performance` is
  indistinguishable from `merge`/`git-workflow`. That did not matter while colour was keyed at the root
  — both collapse there — and it matters the moment it is not: focusing `memory-trace` would have drawn
  `memory-trace-ui` as a second swatch for one concept, in the one view whose job is to make the
  distinctions honest. The map already existed server-side as `index.resolution()`; only the exposure
  was missing. 12 aliases are authored on this corpus, so this is a live case, not a hypothetical.
- A: Infer alias-ness client-side (e.g. "not in the wheel") — rejected: the wheel holds only qualifying
  topics, so the test would be wrong for every rare canonical slug.
- F: `memory-trace/memory_trace/service.py`, `models.py`, `tests/contract/openapi.v1.json`,
  `tests/contract/types.ts`.
- T: 175 client unit tests (8 new on the focus rules — distinct children, unchanged outside, floor
  waived, lone child derives its parent, alias collapses, child-focus expands its family, legend agrees),
  7 story tests, 213 memory-trace backend tests, typecheck clean, contract regenerated, build OK. Live:
  focusing `git-workflow` took the palette from 20 to 28 distinct node colours.

### Follow-up

- The left-hand topic chips are NOT filtered to roots — they are `Object.entries(facets.topics)
  .slice(0, 10)`, i.e. the ten most-authored slugs. They are all roots today only because every child
  carries 1-6 entries; a child crossing into the top ten after the sweep would displace a root and the
  pane would mix levels without saying so. If roots-only is the intent, it needs to be stated in code.
- The community FLOOR is waived inside a focus but the community assignment is not: the legend still
  lists server-named communities, which are root-level, so a focused view shows child colours against
  parent-named legend rows. Not wrong, but the legend is now less informative than the map it explains.

### Summary

- Built proposal mode and ran it on `memory-trace`. It rejected the split, and the rejection is the
  finding: half the concentration is not missing children at all.

### Decisions

#### D1 - Proposal mode is two read-only commands that do not talk to each other

- D: `scripts/propose_topic_children.py` with `gather` (dump the entries under a slug, with the
  co-occurring topics that mark the likely seams) and `score` (take a candidate split, compute the
  projected share, the floor test, the leftover coverage). Neither can write `topics.yaml`.
- R: The design's whole safety argument is that assignment and proposal never merge: assignment writes
  and is bounded by a closed vocabulary, so proposal can afford to be speculative precisely because it
  cannot write. Splitting the tool in two carries that into the implementation — `gather` produces the
  material a judgement is made FROM and makes none itself; `score` can say what a slug would do but
  cannot create one. The floor (8 entries) and the target parent share (20%) live in code rather than in
  a reviewer's head, so a proposal can FAIL cleanly instead of being argued about.
- A: One command that clusters and proposes — rejected: it would put the judgement inside the tool,
  where the reviewer cannot see it, and make a speculative step look like a computed one.
- F: `scripts/propose_topic_children.py`.

#### D2 - The first proposal is REJECTED, and the reason is a redundant tag rather than a missing child

- D: The four candidates that clear the floor (`trail` 40, `trace-shell` 14, `trace-cache` 12,
  `trace-harness` 10) are recorded as a proposal for JNL, and the split is scored REJECT.
- R: The scorer refused it on its own stated rules — three candidates below the floor, and the parent
  landing at 24.3% against a 20% target. Chasing that gap turned up the real shape: **86 of the 202
  `memory-trace` entries (43%) already carry a finer AREA slug**, 63 of them `graph`. For those the
  parent is not under-specified, it is redundant, and no child can fix a tag that should not be there.
  Children alone take it 43.9% → 24.3%; children plus retracting those 86 take it to **8.7%**. So the
  concentration is roughly half a missing-children problem and half a redundant-tag problem, and only
  the first half is a vocabulary question.
  A `graph-view` child was deliberately not proposed for the same reason: 63 of these entries already
  carry the `graph` root, so a Trace-flavoured duplicate would be exactly the unearned depth the
  promotion rule refuses.
- A: Lower the floor so `inspector` (7), `diagram-view` (4) and `trace-api` (3) qualify — rejected: the
  floor is the only thing standing between "earned depth" and a vocabulary that grows whenever someone
  wants a finer label. `inspector` is one entry short and will qualify on its own soon enough.
- F: `docs/2_Todo/memory-trace-children-proposal.md`.
- T: `docs check` OK across 184 files. Every number in the proposal is reproducible from the two
  commands; nothing was written to `topics.yaml`.

### Follow-up

- **Two decisions are now with JNL**, and neither is an agent's to make: approve the four children
  (a governance change to deploy-once state), and rule on whether `memory-trace` should come off entries
  that already carry a finer area slug.
- The second of those needs a **topics `retracts:`** construct that does not exist. Links have one;
  topics do not, so there is currently no legal way to remove a topic an author wrote. That is now on
  the critical path to the sweep rather than a tidy-up item — it is the larger half of the largest
  concentration in the vocabulary.
- Clustering was keyword-led over titles and then reviewed, not a swarm pass over the bodies. Weaker
  evidence than the design asks for, and stated as such in the proposal: 26 of the 116 matched no
  pattern at all, and those boundaries are the ones a swarm would firm up.
