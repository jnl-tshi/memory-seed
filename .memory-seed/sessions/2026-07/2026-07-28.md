### Summary

- The sidecar now declares `area:` and `activity:` structurally instead of leaving the axis to be
  derived, and decision rows stop inheriting their entry's rollup.

### Decisions

#### D1 - The axis is declared, not derived

- D: Topic sidecars nest under the axis:
  `topics:` / `  area:` / `    - graph:d1` / `  activity:` / `    - bugfix:d1`.
  The reader accepts both shapes and exposes `decision_area` / `decision_activity` beside the existing
  `decision_topics`. All 43 sidecars regenerated; 653 of 686 entries carry a declared area.
- R: JNL's design. It makes "one area and one activity per decision" a SHAPE rather than a rule someone
  has to remember to check, and a reader knows which slug is which without consulting `topics.yaml`.
  **The tradeoff, stated because `topics.yaml` makes the opposite argument about children** ("restating
  an axis would be a second copy of a fact that can drift"): a declared axis CAN drift if a slug is ever
  moved between axes. The mitigation is that drift becomes detectable - the declared axis and
  `axis_of()` can be compared - where a derived-only axis silently follows the vocabulary wherever it
  goes. Verified 0 slugs currently sit in the wrong bucket.
  The nested form is READABLE BY THE OLD READER, which is what makes the migration safe both ways: a
  flat reader collects the same `- ` lines and sees a correct if axis-blind list rather than nothing.
- A: Derive the axis at read time and keep the flat list - rejected by JNL; it leaves the pair implicit
  exactly where the pair is the point.
- F: `memory_seed/retrieval.py` (`entry_topic_sidecars`), 43 regenerated sidecars.
- T: `links check` OK across 136 files; every slug validates against its declared bucket.

#### D2 - Each decision row gets its own area and activity

- D: `_expand_decision_rows` keys each row's attribution off the entry's `inferred_decision_topics` for
  that ordinal, emitting `decision_area` and `decision_activity` plus their union as `topics`. Falls
  back to the anchor's topics when a decision has no attribution.
- R: Rows were built with `dict(node, ...)`, so every one inherited the entry's rolled-up topics and the
  whole group rendered in one colour. **109 entries have decisions whose areas genuinely differ** -
  `ms-845042c7` is `retrieval` on d1/d2 and `agent-rules` on d3 - and all of them averaged the
  distinction away. That is precisely what decision granularity exists to record.
  `inferred_decision_topics` appeared NOWHERE in the memory-trace package before this: the pairs were
  read off disk, attached to the chunk, and never consumed.
  The fallback matters: a row with no attribution rendering colourless would read as a defect rather
  than as missing data.
- F: `memory-trace/memory_trace/service.py` (`_expand_decision_rows`, `_AXIS_OF`),
  `memory-trace/tests/test_topic_sidecars.py` (+2).
- T: 227 Trace + 782 Seed tests. Live: `ms-845042c7` now reads d1 `retrieval`/`design-evaluation`,
  d2 `retrieval`/`feature-build`, d3 `agent-rules`/`feature-build` - d1 and d2 differ by ACTIVITY only,
  which is exactly the case pair-colouring makes visible and rollup could never show.

### Follow-up

- **The client cannot colour by the pair yet.** `decision_area`/`decision_activity` reach the service
  payload but the v1 projection route has a typed node schema that drops unknown keys, so they stop at
  the contract. Exposing them is a contract change (`openapi.v1.json`, `types.ts`) plus the client
  palette work - that is the remaining half of JNL's ask.
- Two rows came back `community:unassigned` because their area is below `COMMUNITY_TOPIC_FLOOR`. Correct
  as designed, but worth revisiting once colour keys on the PAIR rather than a single community.
- Still outstanding from JNL: topic DISCOVERY for new projects - swarm workers emitting candidate
  concepts with confidence, aggregated into proposed merges and hierarchies rather than a finalised
  model, with areas treated as a DAG rather than a tree. Not started; it is a design proposal, not a
  code change.

### Summary

- The navigation pane's flat "Topics" chips become an "Ontology" browser: an Areas/Activities toggle over
  a recursive, arbitrarily-deep tree, generated from the vocabulary rather than hardcoded.

### Decisions

#### D1 - A new `ontology` facet, keyed by axis

- D: `TraceService.ontology()` emits `{axis: [OntologyNode, ...]}` where a node is
  `{id, name, count, total, children}` - the same shape at every depth. Added to the `Facets` model and
  the v1 contract; `openapi.v1.json` and `types.ts` regenerated.
- R: The client could not build a tree from what was served. `topic_roots` answers "which root owns this
  slug" and **deliberately collapses everything between**, so there were no parent edges and no axis - a
  navigator has to draw exactly what that map throws away.
  Keyed BY AXIS rather than returned as one forest because the axes answer different questions and are
  navigated one at a time. JNL's requirement that "additional ontology types could be added later by
  extending the toggle" is then literally true: a third axis is a third key, and the toggle is built from
  `Object.keys`.
  **`total` rather than `count` is what the UI shows.** Selecting a node returns its whole subtree, so
  printing the node's own count beside a parent whose children hold most of the corpus would make the
  number disagree with the result it produces. `memory-trace` reads 200, not 78.
  Aliases are excluded: they are spellings, not concepts, and listing `memory-trace-ui` beside
  `memory-trace` offers two doors into one room.
- F: `memory-trace/memory_trace/service.py`, `models.py`, `tests/contract/{openapi.v1.json,types.ts}`.
- T: Live `/api/v1/facets` serves 18 area roots and 12 activity roots, three levels deep
  (`memory-trace > panes > inspector`).

#### D2 - A generic TreeView that knows nothing about topics

- D: `TreeView.tsx` renders any `{id, name, children}` recursively. Expansion state is owned by the
  CALLER, not the component. Chevron and label are separate buttons.
- R: JNL asked for a reusable component that needs no modification when the ontology changes, so it takes
  the tree and the callbacks and holds no domain knowledge at all.
  **Expansion lives outside** for two reasons: switching axis must not forget where you were (a component
  holding its own state resets on remount), and it lets the caller open an ancestor chain when a
  selection arrives from elsewhere - `ancestorIdsOf` does exactly that, so choosing a topic on the graph
  reveals it in the tree instead of highlighting a row folded out of sight.
  **Chevron and label are separate controls** because expanding a branch to look inside it is a different
  intent from filtering the graph to it. One control doing both makes the cheaper action impossible.
- A: One button toggling both - rejected on the above. Component-owned expansion - rejected: it loses
  state on axis switch, which is the one thing JNL asked to preserve.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/TreeView.tsx` (new), `App.tsx`, `styles.css`.
- T: 227 Trace + 782 Seed tests, `tsc` clean, 182 client tests.

### Follow-up

- **Verified live rather than assumed**, on a spare port so JNL's own preview was untouched. Selecting a
  parent includes every descendant at every depth: `trail` (47) and `panes` (48) are both subsets of
  `memory-trace` (186), and `inspector` (9) is a subset of `panes`. That behaviour was already correct
  server-side - `expand_topic_filter` matches a parent against its subtree - so the tree needed to change
  nothing about filtering, only to expose the shape.
- Two things the first pass got wrong and measurement caught: the axis toggle sorted alphabetically, so
  **Activities** came first purely by spelling (fixed - area first, then activity, then anything else);
  and my DOM check counted 40 roots because `.tree > .tree-item` matches at every nesting level. The
  real answer was 18.
- The flat chip list survives as a FALLBACK for a vocabulary that declares no axes - a project without a
  hierarchy still needs to filter.
- Expansion is persisted as one flat set of slugs shared across axes. Slugs are unique across axes, so a
  slug that later moves or disappears simply stops matching.
