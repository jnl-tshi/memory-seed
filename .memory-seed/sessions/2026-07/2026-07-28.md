### Summary

- The sidecar now declares `area:` and `activity:` structurally instead of leaving the axis to be
  derived, and decision rows stop inheriting their entry's rollup.

### Decisions

#### D1 - The axis is declared, not derived

- D: Topic sidecars nest under the axis:
  `topics:` / `  area:` / `    - graph:d1` / `  activity:` / `    - bugfix:d1`.
  The reader accepts both shapes and exposes `decision_area` / `decision_activity` beside the existing
  `decision_topics`. All 43 sidecars regenerated; 653 of 686 entries carry a declared area.
- R: JNL's design. It makes "one area and one activity per decision" a SHAPE rather than a rule someone
  has to remember to check, and a reader knows which slug is which without consulting `topics.yaml`.
  **The tradeoff, stated because `topics.yaml` makes the opposite argument about children** ("restating
  an axis would be a second copy of a fact that can drift"): a declared axis CAN drift if a slug is ever
  moved between axes. The mitigation is that drift becomes detectable - the declared axis and

  goes. Verified 0 slugs currently sit in the wrong bucket.
  The nested form is READABLE BY THE OLD READER, which is what makes the migration safe both ways: a
  flat reader collects the same `- ` lines and sees a correct if axis-blind list rather than nothing.
- A: Derive the axis at read time and keep the flat list - rejected by JNL; it leaves the pair implicit
  exactly where the pair is the point.
- F: `memory_seed/retrieval.py` (`entry_topic_sidecars`), 43 regenerated sidecars.
- T: `links check` OK across 136 files; every slug validates against its declared bucket.

#### D2 - Each decision row gets its own area and activity

- D: `_expand_decision_rows` keys each row's attribution off the entry's `inferred_decision_topics` for
  that ordinal, emitting `decision_area` and `decision_activity` plus their union as `topics`. Falls
  back to the anchor's topics when a decision has no attribution.
- R: Rows were built with `dict(node, ...)`, so every one inherited the entry's rolled-up topics and the
  whole group rendered in one colour. **109 entries have decisions whose areas genuinely differ** -
  `ms-845042c7` is `retrieval` on d1/d2 and `agent-rules` on d3 - and all of them averaged the
  distinction away. That is precisely what decision granularity exists to record.
  `inferred_decision_topics` appeared NOWHERE in the memory-trace package before this: the pairs were
  read off disk, attached to the chunk, and never consumed.
  The fallback matters: a row with no attribution rendering colourless would read as a defect rather
  than as missing data.
- F: `memory-trace/memory_trace/service.py` (`_expand_decision_rows`, `_AXIS_OF`),
  `memory-trace/tests/test_topic_sidecars.py` (+2).
- T: 227 Trace + 782 Seed tests. Live: `ms-845042c7` now reads d1 `retrieval`/`design-evaluation`,

### Summary

- The navigation pane's flat "Topics" chips become an "Ontology" browser: an Areas/Activities toggle over
  a recursive, arbitrarily-deep tree, generated from the vocabulary rather than hardcoded.

### Decisions

#### D1 - A new `ontology` facet, keyed by axis

- D: `TraceService.ontology()` emits `{axis: [OntologyNode, ...]}` where a node is
  `{id, name, count, total, children}` - the same shape at every depth. Added to the `Facets` model and
  the v1 contract; `openapi.v1.json` and `types.ts` regenerated.
- R: The client could not build a tree from what was served. `topic_roots` answers "which root owns this
  slug" and **deliberately collapses everything between**, so there were no parent edges and no axis - a
  navigator has to draw exactly what that map throws away.
  Keyed BY AXIS rather than returned as one forest because the axes answer different questions and are
  navigated one at a time. JNL's requirement that "additional ontology types could be added later by
  extending the toggle" is then literally true: a third axis is a third key, and the toggle is built from
  `Object.keys`.
  **`total` rather than `count` is what the UI shows.** Selecting a node returns its whole subtree, so
  printing the node's own count beside a parent whose children hold most of the corpus would make the
  number disagree with the result it produces. `memory-trace` reads 200, not 78.
  Aliases are excluded: they are spellings, not concepts, and listing `memory-trace-ui` beside
  `memory-trace` offers two doors into one room.
- F: `memory-trace/memory_trace/service.py`, `models.py`, `tests/contract/{openapi.v1.json,types.ts}`.
- T: Live `/api/v1/facets` serves 18 area roots and 12 activity roots, three levels deep
  (`memory-trace > panes > inspector`).

#### D2 - A generic TreeView that knows nothing about topics

- D: `TreeView.tsx` renders any `{id, name, children}` recursively. Expansion state is owned by the
  CALLER, not the component. Chevron and label are separate buttons.
- R: JNL asked for a reusable component that needs no modification when the ontology changes, so it takes
  the tree and the callbacks and holds no domain knowledge at all.
  **Expansion lives outside** for two reasons: switching axis must not forget where you were (a component
  holding its own state resets on remount), and it lets the caller open an ancestor chain when a
  selection arrives from elsewhere - `ancestorIdsOf` does exactly that, so choosing a topic on the graph
  reveals it in the tree instead of highlighting a row folded out of sight.
  **Chevron and label are separate controls** because expanding a branch to look inside it is a different
  intent from filtering the graph to it. One control doing both makes the cheaper action impossible.
- A: One button toggling both - rejected on the above. Component-owned expansion - rejected: it loses
  state on axis switch, which is the one thing JNL asked to preserve.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/TreeView.tsx` (new), `App.tsx`, `styles.css`.
- T: 227 Trace + 782 Seed tests, `tsc` clean, 182 client tests.

### Follow-up

- **Verified live rather than assumed**, on a spare port so JNL's own preview was untouched. Selecting a
  parent includes every descendant at every depth: `trail` (47) and `panes` (48) are both subsets of
  `memory-trace` (186), and `inspector` (9) is a subset of `panes`. That behaviour was already correct
  server-side - `expand_topic_filter` matches a parent against its subtree - so the tree needed to change
  nothing about filtering, only to expose the shape.
- Two things the first pass got wrong and measurement caught: the axis toggle sorted alphabetically, so
  **Activities** came first purely by spelling (fixed - area first, then activity, then anything else);
  and my DOM check counted 40 roots because `.tree > .tree-item` matches at every nesting level. The
  real answer was 18.
- The flat chip list survives as a FALLBACK for a vocabulary that declares no axes - a project without a
  hierarchy still needs to filter.
- Expansion is persisted as one flat set of slugs shared across axes. Slugs are unique across axes, so a
  slug that later moves or disappears simply stops matching.

## 2026-07-28 10:40 - Long lifecycle chains wind into a spiral, oldest innermost

### Summary

- A per-chain radial spring winds long `evolves`/`replaces` threads into a spiral carrying time, and the
  dense sections are protected by construction rather than by tuning.

### Decisions

#### D1 - A radial spring per chain, not a drawn spiral

- D: `graphSpiral.ts` decides which nodes belong to a long lifecycle chain and what radius each should
  hold; a custom d3 force pulls each member toward its own chain's centroid until it sits at that
  radius. The spiral is not drawn anywhere.
- R: JNL asked for long chains to aggregate into a spiral so the middle reads as older. Radius from age
  order plus the existing link force is enough: members want increasing radii around a shared centre
  while consecutive members are still pulled together, and the only shape satisfying both is a winding
  one. Nothing has to lay anything out.
  Written as a CUSTOM force rather than `d3.forceRadial` because that takes one centre for the whole
  simulation - it would wind every chain around the middle of the map and pile them on each other.
  Chains need their own centres, recomputed as they drift.
  Radius follows age ORDER, not elapsed time: a gap-scaled radius would leave a quiet fortnight as a
  visible void, which reads as a missing node rather than as elapsed time.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphSpiral.ts` (new), `GraphWorkspace.tsx`, `graphForces.ts`,
  `SettingsMenu.tsx`, `graphSpiral.test.ts` (new, 11 tests).
- T: Measured headlessly against the real 691-node graph, run twice from an identical start with the
  force on and off. The qualifying chain: **89% age-ordered, radius 71 -> 542, 633 degrees of sweep** -
  nearly two turns, oldest innermost.

#### D2 - MAX degree is what protects the dense sections, and a test caught that it was mean

- D: A component qualifies only if it is >= 8 nodes, max degree <= 3, and mean degree <= 2.4. Everything
  else gets **no radial force at all** - not a weak one.
- R: JNL's constraint was that this must not cost the denser sections. Per-node exclusion makes that
  structural rather than a matter of tuning: a node outside a chain is governed by exactly the forces it
  was before.
  **The first version used MEAN degree and was wrong.** Any tree has mean degree 2(n-1)/n - just under
  2 - so a 30-spoke hub scores 1.94 and a straight chain scores 1.67. The mean cannot tell them apart,
  and the rule would have wound every hub in the corpus into a ring. The unit test for a star is what
  caught it; on the live corpus the 289-node component has max degree 24 and is now correctly rejected.
- A: Damp non-chain nodes weakly instead of excluding them - rejected: "weakly wrong everywhere" is
  worse than "right where it applies", and it makes the safety property untestable.
- T: 5 chains qualified at a floor of 5; 1 at a floor of 8. Non-chain mean displacement **129 against a
  graph radius of 2507 (~5%)** - the layout shifts, the structure does not break.

#### D3 - The floor went 5 -> 8 because the measurement said the short chains were noise

- D: `MIN_CHAIN_LENGTH = 8`.
- R: At 5 the probe showed the long chain at 89% age-ordered with a 633-degree sweep, and the five- and
  six-node chains at 40-50% ordered - **no better than chance**, two of them wound inward. The cause is
  geometric, not tunable: a chain's centre is its own members' centroid, so with five members the centre
  sits among them and "inner versus outer" is barely defined.
  Seeding the initial angle (`spiralSeedOffsets`) fixed the long chain - a force layout keeps the
  topology it starts with - and did not rescue the short ones. Raising the floor to where the effect is
  real is the honest response; applying it to short chains bought noise and called it a feature.
- F: `graphSpiral.ts`, and the tests re-pinned to the new floor.

### Follow-up

- **Only one chain qualifies on this corpus today.** That is the honest scope: the force earns its place
  on long threads, and long threads are what JNL asked about. More will qualify as lifecycle edges
  accumulate.
- A "Chain spiral" slider sits beside the other three forces; 0 disables it entirely.
- The probe that produced these numbers was a throwaway (`spiral-probe.mjs`, deleted). Running the
  simulation twice from an identical seed - once with the force, once without - is the only way to say
  what the force itself changed, and it is worth rebuilding if the tuning is ever revisited.

## 2026-07-28 12:20 - Terminating spurs hang off the spine instead of bending the spiral


### Summary

- A chain is now a SPINE plus SPURS. Only the spine is wound; a terminating side-node is pushed outward
  past its anchor and contributes nothing to the shape.

### Decision

- D: `chainSpine()` finds the longest path through a component by double breadth-first search. Spine
  members take the spiral seats; anything off it is marked `spur`, given its anchor's angle and one step
  MORE radius, and **excluded from the chain's centroid**.
- R: JNL's screenshot circled two side-nodes distorting a spiral. Both halves of the fix matter and they
  fix different things:
  **Excluded from the centroid** is what stops a spur bending the shape. Every member's radius is
  measured from that centroid, so one node hanging off to the side pulled the centre toward itself and
  every other member's position with it.
  **Anchor's angle, one step further out** is what makes it point AWAY from the centre. Without the extra
  radius the link force parks it wherever it happens to land - often inside the coil, where it reads as
  part of the sequence rather than as a leaf off it.
  Double BFS is exact on a tree, and these components are near-trees by construction (mean degree <= 2.4),
  so a cycle can only make the spine slightly short - never wrong in a way that matters, since anything
  off the returned path is treated as a spur anyway.
  The spine is oriented oldest-first by looking up its two endpoints in the component's existing
  age-ordered array, so no second date sort is needed.
- A: Drop spurs from the chain entirely - rejected: they are real entries and would vanish from the
  layout's structure. Give them a weak radial pull - rejected for the same reason the force excludes
  non-chain nodes outright: "weakly wrong" is harder to reason about than "not applied".
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphSpiral.ts` (`chainSpine`, `spur` on the assignment),
  `GraphWorkspace.tsx` (spine-only centroid), `graphSpiral.test.ts` (+5).
- T: 202 client tests, 227 Trace tests. On the real corpus **every qualifying chain has spurs** - the
  15-node one is 13 spine + 2 hanging off, which is exactly the pair JNL circled. After the change all
  **2 of 2 spurs settle outside their anchor**.

### Follow-up

- **The spine metrics moved and the comparison is not apples-to-apples.** Age-ordering reads 81% over 13
  spine nodes where it read 89% over all 15, and the sweep 460 degrees where it was 633. Both are far
  above chance, but the centroid is now computed from a different set, so the radii are measured from a
  different origin - this is a different measurement, not a regression in the same one. The outer radius
  also shrinks simply because a 13-node spine is shorter than a 15-node one.
- A spur inherits its anchor's `index`, so two spurs on the same anchor land on the same ray at the same
  radius and will overlap. Not seen on this corpus - every anchor has at most one - and worth a fan-out
  if a corpus ever produces it.

## 2026-07-28 14:53 - Related edges become a second, lower-priority chain kind, gated by whether their topology tracks time

```yaml
entry_id: mse_rrf3kh583sk3zhye
user_initials: JNL

agent_name: Claude Opus 5
project_path: .
subproject_path: null
branch: claude/feature/related-chain-kind
topics:

  - feature-build
related_entries:

```

### Summary

- The chain JNL circled was drawn in `related` edges, which the spiral force never considered. Widening
  it to include `related` surfaced a second, more fundamental problem: unlike lifecycle edges, `related`
  carries no temporal direction, so a topologically valid chain can still get the timeline backwards. A
  concordance gate now catches that specifically - and it excludes the exact chain that was circled, for
  a measured reason rather than a guess.

### Decisions

#### D1 - `related` is a second kind-group, tried only over nodes lifecycle chains did not already claim

- D: `RELATED_CHAIN_KINDS = {"related"}`, tried after `LIFECYCLE_CHAIN_KINDS` in `CHAIN_KIND_GROUPS`. New
  entry point `allSpiralAssignments(nodes, edges, options)` runs each group over only the nodes earlier
  groups left unclaimed, and merges the seats. `spiralChains`/`spiralAssignments` both gained a `kinds`
  option (default lifecycle, unchanged), so every existing call site and all 202 prior tests kept working
  with zero changes.
- R: Diagnosed first: the circled chain rendered solid tan with arrowheads, which is `related`'s colour
  (`--edge-related`), not `evolves` (blue) or `replaces` (red). `CHAIN_EDGE_KINDS` had always excluded
  `related` on purpose.
  Measured before implementing anything: merging `related` into the SAME connectivity graph as lifecycle
  edges produces one ~500-node component (many nodes carry both kinds of edge to overlapping neighbours),
  which the degree caps correctly reject - the merge bought nothing, at any degree threshold. Evaluated
  as its OWN graph, `related` looks nothing like that: one dominant blob (correctly rejected) plus several
  genuinely path-shaped components, including the exact 15-node chain JNL circled (max degree 3, mean
  degree 1.87) and an 8-node one.
  Priority-with-exclusion (not a merged pool, not a weak fallback) is what stops a `related` thread from
  reassigning a node that already has an authored home: a node's lifecycle seat is set first and simply
  removed from the `related` group's candidate pool, so `related` connectivity is computed only among
  what remains - which may shrink or split a `related` component, and that is the intended behaviour.
- A: A single merged edge-kind set with a stricter degree cap - rejected by measurement: no cap setting
  produces anything but "0 new chains" or "one huge blob", because `related`'s aggregate density is what
  it is regardless of where the knob sits.
- F: `memory-trace/client/src/graphSpiral.ts` (kind-groups, `allSpiralAssignments`),
  `GraphWorkspace.tsx` (wired to the new entry point), `graphSpiral.test.ts` (+7).
- T: 214 (then 209 mid-step) tests pass throughout; typecheck clean at every stage.

#### D2 - A concordance gate: shape says a chain is thread-like, it says nothing about whether it tracks time

- D: `MIN_SPINE_CONCORDANCE = 0.85`. After degree checks pass, `spiralChains` walks the candidate's
  spine and computes the fraction of member PAIRS whose spine order agrees with their timestamp order
  (best of the two directions); below the floor, the whole component is dropped.
- R: Found by re-measuring after D1 shipped, not assumed. The 8-node related chain spiraled well (87%
  simulated ordering). The circled 15-node one did not: 53% simulated, worse than the physics even
  started with. Traced to the cause rather than left as "the physics is noisy": its topological
  spine-to-date concordance is only 76% ANALYTICALLY - the spine's early section walks backward through
  a whole day (indices 0-2 are chronologically AFTER indices 3-5) - because `evolves`/`replaces` are
  authored FROM an older entry TO a newer one (topology IS chronology, by construction), while `related`
  records topical similarity with no temporal direction at all. A component can be perfectly path-shaped
  by degree and still not tell time.
  This matters because the whole feature's premise is "the middle is older" (JNL's original ask). A
  spiral that gets that wrong on a quarter of its members is not a rough approximation, it is actively
  misleading - worse than the plain unwound line it replaces, because a viewer would trust the
  convention. 0.85 sits with real margin either side of the two measured cases that must land on
  opposite sides (76% fail, 95%/100% pass).
- A: Ship D1 without a quality gate, accepting whatever `related` produces - rejected once the 53%
  measurement surfaced; a feature whose entire value proposition is encoding time cannot ship a case
  that inverts it on the visible example. Drop `related` after all - rejected: the 8-node case shows it
  is not a universal problem, only ungated inclusion is.
- F: `graphSpiral.ts` (`spineConcordance`, the gate inside `spiralChains`),
  `graphSpiral.test.ts` (+5, including a synthetic zig-zag fixture and a direct threshold override test).
- T: End-to-end on the live corpus: 3 chains found before the gate (15+8+15), 2 after (15+8) - the
  circled chain excluded, the clean related chain and the original lifecycle chain both kept. Re-ran the
  full geometry+safety probe post-gate: both survivors settle at 83%/80% age-ordered with clean sweeps,
  and non-chain displacement dropped further (105.6, down from 129-159 in earlier measurements) since
  fewer nodes are now under any radial force at all.

### Follow-up

- **The circled chain will still not spiral, and that is the correct outcome, not an unmet request.**
  JNL asked for the encouragement to happen without costing correctness elsewhere; the measured
  alternative - winding a chain that gets its own premise wrong 24-47% of the time - would have been the
  cost. Worth saying plainly if asked why: the fix that was shipped is "make winding depend on whether it
  would be honest," not "make more things wind."
  If the CIRCLED chain in particular is wanted, it would need `MIN_SPINE_CONCORDANCE` overridden low
  (measured: 0 readmits it) or a different signal for the story than raw graph topology - neither
  attempted here.
- **Visual confirmation in the running app was not obtained**, and that is stated rather than implied.
  The search box does not resolve a raw `mse_*`/`ms-*` id to a jump-to-node action (only title/full-text
  match), and loading the full 694-node Overview to hunt for a specific shape risked the known "Show
  more" slowdown for a benefit the headless check already gave with more precision. Verification instead
  ran the actual shipped functions (`allSpiralAssignments`, `spiralChains`, `chainSpine`,
  `spineConcordance`, `spiralSeedOffsets`) imported directly from source, against the live corpus fetched
  from the running API, with the identical d3-force math the client runs - cross-checked three ways
  (analytical concordance, post-simulation position concordance, unit tests) rather than asserted once.
- Two throwaway probe scripts were used and deleted (`related-probe.mjs`, `group-geom-probe.mjs`),
  matching the pattern from the original chain-spiral work.
