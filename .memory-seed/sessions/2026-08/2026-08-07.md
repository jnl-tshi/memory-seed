### Decisions

#### D1 - Found ADRs from control-file lines, not synthetic session entries

- D: Every campaign ADR is founded on the index/policy line it lifts, carrying that line's verbatim
  quote as grounding, with session decisions attached as supporting evidence where they exist.
  `source: derived` throughout. Two decided-and-rejected concerns were written as `rejected`-status

- R: The alternative I had planned - authoring a synthetic "founding" session decision per lifted
  bullet - manufactures provenance for a decision that was actually made elsewhere, and would not
  work for S3 at all, since bootstrap runs on a project with no session corpus. Founding on the
  control-file line records what is true: the decision's current home is that line, and the ADR now
  supersedes it as the place the reasoning lives. Recording the two rejections matters because an
  ADR corpus holding only adopted decisions loses precisely the reasoning most likely to be
  re-litigated.
- A: Synthetic founding entries (rejected - false provenance). Skipping concerns with no locatable
  session decision (rejected - would have dropped roughly two thirds of the architecture).
- F: `experiments/adr-campaign/{concerns.json,write_proposed.py,FINDINGS.md}`,
  `.memory-seed/decisions/*.md` (35 new).
- T: `adr check` green after every batch; the three sha256-pinned ADRs byte-unchanged against
  `real-current.json`; 1086 tests pass.

#### D2 - The swarm's two failure modes were brief defects, and the fix was a closed candidate list

- D: Brief v2 separates the topic vocabulary from the constitution-anchor vocabulary, caps quotes
  at 40-120 characters within a single line, and renders VERIFIED candidate decision refs into each
  worker payload so attachments are chosen from a closed list rather than recalled.
- R: Run 1 lost 12 of 34 ADRs and 15 of 17 attachments. Every drop traced to my brief: 6 workers
  put constitution slugs in `topics` because both vocabularies arrived in one payload with the same
  shape, and 6 quotes matched their opening clause exactly then drifted in the tail, which is what
  wrapping does to a long transcription. The attachment failures were invented or truncated entry
  ids - workers recalling refs instead of reading them, which no instruction fixes but a closed
  list removes. Run 2 recovered 12/12 ADRs and 20/20 attachments.
- A: Blaming the model tier and escalating alone (rejected - it would have shipped the same broken
  brief at higher cost).
- F: `experiments/adr-campaign/{worker-brief-v1.md,worker-brief-v2.md,validate_drafts.py}`.
- T: Run 1 22/34 ADRs and 2/17 attachments; run 2 12/12 and 20/20.

#### D3 - Thin the index only where a bullet is not bundling uncovered decisions

- D: Index bullets under 400 characters thin to a descriptive pointer (one-line digest plus ADR
  link); longer bullets keep their text and gain the ADR reference; policy rules always keep their
  text inline and gain only the governing-ADR pointer. Three bullets whose grounding quotes span

- R: Policy is Always Read, so an agent must learn how to behave without chasing links - that is

- A: Thinning every located bullet (rejected - silent loss). Thinning nothing (rejected - leaves

### Summary

- Built the decision-level lineage map JNL asked for: every ADR member walked forward through
  link-sidecar `evolves`/`replaces` edges to the decision that is current, with `related` edges
  collected as context. 313 edges indexed, 11 unusable after normalisation, 5 ADRs affected.
- Applying the map automatically produced a WRONG head and was reverted before publication. The
  corrected rule - only an authored edge may move a head - yields zero automatic changes and a
  1-item review queue.
- Also fixed a validator defect that would have stranded every founded ADR at its founding
  placeholder forever.

### Decisions

#### D1 - A real decision supersedes a founding head without proving descent

- D: `validate_adr` no longer requires a new head to descend from the current authoritative head
  when that head is a `founding:` pseudo-ref. Descent is still required between two real decisions,
  held by a test asserting an unlinked successor is refused.
- R: A founding ref is a placeholder for "this concern as recorded in the control file", not a node
  in the lineage graph, so no real decision can ever descend from it. The rule as written made the
  convergence the founding mechanism exists for structurally impossible, stranding all 34 campaign
  ADRs at their founding head permanently. Probed before designing: `revise` succeeded and
  `transition` failed with "does not descend from authoritative decision founding:...", locating
  the defect in the acceptance rule rather than the writers.
- A: Landing reconciliations as `proposed` and leaving acceptance broken (rejected - that hands
  over proposals nobody can accept). Making the founding ref a synthetic predecessor (rejected -
  `LINK_ASSERTION_RE` requires real decision refs at both ends, so it would mean loosening the edge
  grammar to patch a status rule).
- F: `memory_seed/adr.py`, `tests/test_adr_contract_extension.py`.
- T: Founding-head convergence accepted end-to-end; descent between two real decisions still
  refused; 31 ADR tests pass.

#### D2 - Only an authored edge may move an ADR head; machine-scored edges go to a review queue

- D: Head changes are applied only when every step of the chain is authored (no `edge_confidence`
  entry). Machine-scored edges are written to `LINEAGE-REVIEW.md` with BOTH decision titles, and
  `reconcile_lineage.py` applies nothing unless the ADR is named with `--approve`. Batch mode was
  removed.
- R: Measured, not assumed. The first pass applied the map wholesale under a confidence floor of
  0.7 and moved `adr_subproject_scoping` onto `ms-0bd3d8b2:d1` - "Use a seeded trigger registry
  instead of only prose triggers" - a decision about skill triggers, not sub-project scoping,
  reached through a single 0.75-scored machine edge. Reverted before publication. Raising the floor
  would not fix it: the same corpus offers 0.9-scored candidates with the same failure mode, and
  the link campaign's own premise is that the swarm suggests while a human decides. An ADR head is
  exactly the kind of decision that reserves. Showing both titles side by side is what exposed the
  error, so the queue leads with them.
- A: A confidence floor alone (rejected - measured to fail at 0.75 and unfixable by raising it).
  Auto-applying and letting review catch it later (rejected - the ledger is append-only, so a wrong
  accepted head is corrected only by more events).
- F: `experiments/adr-campaign/lineage_map.py`, `reconcile_lineage.py`, `lineage-map.json`,
  `LINEAGE-REVIEW.md`.
- T: 4 authored lineage edges touch ADR members and all 4 confirm heads the pinned ADRs already
  carry; 8 machine-scored edges touch members; 0 automatic changes, 1 ADR queued for review.

#### D3 - Stop on fan-out, not on hop count

- D: The walk follows a chain only while a node has exactly one qualifying successor, stopping at
  any branch; bare refs normalise to `<entry>:dN` only where the entry has exactly one decision.
- R: An unrestricted 6-hop walk from `adr_control_file_authority` reached the decision-identity
  mandate, which belongs to a different ADR. Hop limits cap drift without preventing it, whereas a
  branch is the actual signal that the concern split - and choosing a branch is judgement rather
  than mechanics.
- A: A fixed hop limit (rejected - drifts within the limit).
- F: `experiments/adr-campaign/lineage_map.py`.
- T: 313 edges indexed, 11 unusable, 0 ambiguous walks after the fan-out rule.

### Facts

- The map CONFIRMS all three sha256-pinned ADRs: their members' authored chains terminate at the

- The lineage payoff is thin because attachment is thin - only 24 session decisions are attached
