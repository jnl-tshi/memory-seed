### Summary

- Set out to re-measure the memory-index dry-run after the ranking rebuild. Did not spend the ~$25,
  because a free pre-check showed the instrument does not measure ranking.
- The expected answers live in `.memory-seed/index.md`; `memory_search` indexes only
  `.memory-seed/sessions/**`; `AGENTS.md` tells the agent to read `index.md` as step 2 of
  orientation. The blended score is carried by summary-file reading.
- The BM25F change shows no small-corpus regression on the search path, which was the risk worth
  checking.

### Decisions

#### D1 - Do not run the end-to-end re-quiz; the instrument cannot see the change

- D: Stop after the free phase. Record the finding and leave run 3's numbers as the last word,
  rather than adding a run 4 that would be misread.
- R: I recommended this re-run as the thing that would show whether the day's work was worth it, and
  that recommendation was wrong twice over. First, the instrument is saturated: run 3 scored 30/31,
  so the blended number can move by at most one question. Second and decisively, it does not measure
  retrieval at all. The answers to the quiz - "Marcus owns the benchmark suite", "Dana is lead
  maintainer", the 1.2us baseline - sit in `index.md`, which is not in the searchable corpus, and
  the fixture's own `AGENTS.md` instructs the agent to read that file during orientation. A re-run
  would therefore have returned roughly 96.8 whatever the ranker did, and that null was primed to be
  read as "the ranking rebuild did no harm" when the truthful reading is "the ranking rebuild was
  not on the critical path". Twenty-five dollars for a number that would have misinformed us is a
  worse outcome than no number.
- A: Running it anyway as a regression check was declined - the columns it would have moved are not
  the ones ranking touches, so a stable score would have been evidence of nothing. Re-seeding a
  fresh corpus first was declined as a larger piece of work that this finding reshapes: the question
  to ask of a new fixture is now different.
- F: `experiments/memory-index-dryrun/answer_visible.py`.
- T: Phase 1 gate CLEAR - answer-visible identical under both configurations at k=1/3/8. The
  configurations were confirmed to differ before that null was accepted (same query, different
  order and different scores), and the insensitivity was traced to the store holding 12 decision
  chunks against a top-8 window, so the top 8 contain 8 of 12 either way.

#### D2 - Re-read what run 3's +22.6 actually bought

- D: Record that the dry-run's jump from 74.2 to 96.8 is attributable to routing durable facts into
  `index.md` at capture time, not to retrieval quality.
- R: The plan document already states decision-level retrieval was worth +3.2 of the total, and this
  run explains the rest: once a fact is in `index.md` the agent reads it directly and no ranking is
  involved. That matters for how the score is described externally. It is a fair measure of the
  product - agents really do read that file - but it is not evidence about the retrieval layer, and
  the Verging Labs comparison in the same document sits next to entrants whose scores are recall
  measurements. Quoting 96.8 as a retrieval result would overclaim.
- A: Leaving the attribution implicit was declined; the number is already in a strategy document and
  the misreading is one step away.
- F: `docs/2_Todo/memory-index-dry-run-plan.md`, `experiments/memory-index-dryrun/answer_visible.py`.
- T: `grep` of the seeded store for each expected answer, plus the corpus source listing showing
  only `sessions/2026-08/2026-08-05.md` is indexed.

### Facts

- The honest end-to-end test of retrieval is to quiz with `index.md` withheld, so `memory_search` is
  the only route to an answer. That is a new measurement rather than a comparison against run 3, and
  it is the shape the next dry-run should take.
- The seeded workspace holds 10 entries / 12 decision chunks. At that size a top-8 window returns
  most of the store regardless of ordering, so any top-k metric on it is close to insensitive - a
  limit worth knowing before designing another small-fixture experiment.
- The fixture template's `.mcp.json` runs `python -m memory_seed.mcp_server` with `PYTHONPATH`
  pointed at the primary working tree, so spawned sessions load local source and would have picked
  up the ranking changes. That dependency was checked first and was not the problem.

## 2026-08-06 04:30 - Half the corpus is served as a 280-character teaser


### Summary

- Asking `search_memory` for decision granularity does not guarantee decision chunks: an entry with
  no `#### Dn` DRAFT block comes back as an entry chunk, and entry chunks are served as a
  280-character single-line preview instead of the whole block.
- 571 of 1259 chunks on the real corpus (45%), withholding 987,701 characters - 51% of everything
  the store holds. 488 of those chunks are over 1000 characters, so it is not an edge case.
- The fix is not applied: it is a payload-budget decision, and picking a shape without measuring is
  how the constants this session spent the day fixing came to exist.

### Decision

- D: Record the defect and the three candidate fixes in `docs/2_Todo/excerpt-fallback-defect.md`;
  apply none of them yet. Keep `answer_visible.py`'s 7/23 as the recorded "before".
- R: The excerpt branch at `retrieval.py:2625` tests the chunk's ACTUAL granularity rather than the
  granularity the caller asked for, so the whole-DRAFT-block behaviour that shipped on 2026-08-05
  only ever applied to entries that use the DRAFT decision format. Everything else silently kept the
  old preview. The consequence is not "previews are cheap" but "whichever entries happen to lack
  decision headings are invisible", which is arbitrary rather than economical. Serving every entry
  chunk at the 2500 cap would take a top-8 payload from roughly 2k characters to as much as 20k, so
  the right shape is probably a budget across the result set rather than a per-chunk cap - and that
  is a measurement, not a preference. Applying a guess tonight would repeat exactly the pattern the
  rest of the day was spent undoing.
- A: Fixing it immediately with option 1 (serve everything at the decision cap) was declined on the
  payload cost, which nobody has measured. Leaving it unrecorded until there was time to fix it was
  also declined - the finding is worth more than the fix right now, because it changes how the
  dry-run's 96.8 should be read.
- F: `docs/2_Todo/excerpt-fallback-defect.md`, `experiments/memory-index-dryrun/answer_visible.py`.
- T: Measured on both corpora. Before-number for any fix: answer-visible 7/23 at k=8 on the seeded
  store, with all 23 answers confirmed present in the indexed session entries.

### Facts

- The dry-run's dependence on `index.md` is a consequence of this defect, not a design choice. All
  23 answers are in the session entries; retrieval was serving 280 characters of each, so the agent
  had to read the summary file. That is why the blended score is insensitive to ranking.
- Ruling this out took four eliminations in order, each of which looked like the answer: the ranking
  configuration (both tested, orderings genuinely differ), the corpus (all answers present), the
  top-k window (serving all 12 chunks changes nothing), and `DECISION_TEXT_LIMIT` truncation (the
  target terms sit at characters 859-1180, well inside the 2500 cap).
- `_excerpt` also collapses newlines with `" ".join(text.split())`, so an affected result loses its
  structure as well as its length - a DRAFT block arrives as one run-on line.

## 2026-08-06 04:49 - One way to see a decision, and half the corpus becomes visible

```yaml

```

### Summary

- The reader recognised only `#### Dn` as a decision, so the 494 entries using the singular
  `### Decision` form produced no decision chunk, fell back to the whole-entry unit, and were served
  as a 280-character preview. 45% of the corpus, 51% of its text, silently withheld.
- Fixed at the cause rather than by budgeting payload: 571 chunks served at 280 characters down to
  77, withheld text 51% down to 2%, and answer-visible on the seeded fixture 7/23 up to 22/23.
- `memory_get_chunk` also could not fetch a decision chunk at all, which made two shipped behaviours
  dead letters.

### Decisions

#### D1 - The numbered form is the only decision shape; the singular one is legacy

- D: `session_logging.md` (live and seed) now mandates `### Decisions` + `#### D1` regardless of how
  many decisions an entry records. `_find_decision_ranges` recognises the singular `### Decision`
  heading as the same concept, marked LEGACY, so existing entries stay readable.
- R: JNL's reading was that the fault is having two ways of seeing a decision at all, and that is
  the right level. My own framing had been a payload-budget trade-off between serving 2500
  characters and 280, which took the two authoring shapes as given and would have shipped a
  compromise instead of a fix. There was no trade-off to make: once the singular form is recognised
  the fallback almost never fires, so nothing has to be rationed. Numbering every decision also
  makes `entry_id:d1` addressing universal, which lifecycle edges, ADR sidecars and decision-scoped
  topics already assume - the singular form was the one shape those could not name.
- A: Rewriting the 494 existing entries into the numbered form was never on the table: the store is
  append-only. Keeping both shapes as co-equal was declined - that is the state that produced the
  defect, and a reader with two notions of a decision will drift again. Serving entry chunks at the
  decision cap was declined as treating the symptom, and it would have raised a top-8 payload from
  about 2k characters to as much as 20k for no reason.
- F: `memory_seed/semantic_cache.py` (`_LEGACY_SINGLE_DECISION_RE`, `_find_decision_ranges`),
  `.memory-seed/skills/session_logging.md` and its seed twin,
  `docs/2_Todo/excerpt-fallback-defect.md`.
- T: Real corpus, chunks served at 280 characters: 571/1259 -> 77/1259; withheld text 51% -> 2%.
  Seeded fixture answer-visible@8: 7/23 -> 22/23. 1072 tests pass. The remaining 77 are entries with
  no decision section at all, which correctly stay whole and now have a test fixture of their own.

#### D2 - `memory_get_chunk` can fetch a decision chunk

- D: `get_chunk` searches decision granularity after entry and section.
- R: It searched entry then section and stopped, so `mse_x:d1` raised "chunk_id not found". Two
  shipped behaviours depended on it working: the truncation marker on a long decision reads "call
  memory_get_chunk for the full decision", which was an instruction to make a failing call, and the
  attention layer scores on `memory_get_chunk` fetches, so a decision chunk could never register
  one - the ranking signal was unreachable for the granularity that is now the default. Neither
  surfaced as an error anywhere; both were found by trying the call.
- A: Searching decision granularity first was declined - entry and section ids are more common and
  cheaper to match, and this path is on every fetch.
- F: `memory_seed/retrieval.py` (`get_chunk`).
- T: Decision ids returned by `search_memory` now fetch; verified against three.

#### D3 - Two tests were asserting the defect

- D: Replace `test_entry_without_decision_headings_stays_whole` with one asserting the legacy form
  becomes a decision chunk, and add a fixture with genuinely no decision section so the original
  property keeps a home. Make the supersession damping assertion relative rather than absolute.
- R: The first test's fixture is titled "a plain entry with no decision headings" and contains a
  `### Decision` section with D and R bullets - it was a single-decision entry all along, and the
  test had pinned the defect as intended behaviour. Its stated property is still worth holding, so
  it needed a fixture that actually has it rather than deletion. The second compared a damped score
  to six decimal places of an absolute value, so it broke when BM25F changed the scale while the
  damping relationship held exactly; comparing the ratio fixes the class, and five places rather
  than six because the payload rounds scores before serving them.
- A: Loosening the damping tolerance without switching to a ratio was declined - that hides the next
  scale change instead of being immune to it.
- F: `tests/test_decision_retrieval.py`, `tests/test_retrieval.py`.
- T: 1072 pass.

### Facts

- The defect was found by asking why the memory-index dry-run scored 96.8 while retrieval surfaced
  7 of 23 answers, and it took four eliminations that each looked like the answer: ranking
  configuration, corpus contents, the top-k window, and `DECISION_TEXT_LIMIT` truncation.
- `_excerpt` collapses newlines with `" ".join(text.split())`, so an affected result lost its
  structure as well as its length - a DRAFT block arrived as one run-on line.
- The dry-run's dependence on `index.md` was a consequence of this, not a design choice. Whether
  its 96.8 changes now that retrieval can serve whole blocks is unmeasured.

## 2026-08-06 09:32 - A fetched decision arrives with the entry that frames it

```yaml

```

### Summary

- `memory_get_chunk("entry:d1")` now returns `entry_context` alongside the decision block: the
  entry's Summary, Follow-up, Validation, Facts - whatever the author recorded at entry level.
- Sibling decisions are excluded, so asking for `:d1` does not drag `:d2` back in.

### Decision

#### D1 - A decision payload carries its entry's non-decision sections

- D: Add `entry_context` to the `get_chunk` payload for decision chunks - an ordered list of the
  entry's `###` sections excluding the decisions container. Entry-granularity fetches get an empty
  list, since the whole entry already contains them.
- R: JNL's point is that a decision is not self-contained. The D/R/A/F/T block records the choice,
  but the `### Summary` says what the session was doing when it made it and the `### Follow-up` says
  what was left open - both written deliberately, both lost the moment retrieval started serving
  decisions rather than entries. Decision granularity became the default this week, so every fetch
  since has been handing agents a block stripped of its frame. All entry-level sections travel
  rather than a chosen subset, because which of them bears on a given decision is the author's
  judgement expressed in the entry, not something the reader can infer. The decisions container is
  the one exclusion: its body IS the sibling blocks, so including it would return the whole entry
  and defeat the point of asking for one decision.
- A: Merging the sections into `text` was declined - `text` is the decision block and callers rely
  on that; context belongs beside it, labelled, so a reader can tell what the author wrote about
  this decision from what they wrote about the session. Returning only Summary and Follow-up was
  declined as a guess about relevance that the entry format does not license.
- F: `memory_seed/retrieval.py` (`entry_context_sections`, `get_chunk`),
  `memory_seed/mcp_server.py` (tool description), `tests/test_decision_retrieval.py`.
- T: 1075 tests pass, three of them new: context is present and contains Summary, siblings are
  excluded, and entry-granularity fetches stay empty. Verified against the real corpus - a decision
  fetch returns Summary at 548 characters and Facts at 832 with no sibling content.

### Facts

- Three test methods were briefly invisible: appended after `if __name__ == "__main__":`, they sat
  inside that block at matching indentation, so Python accepted them and pytest never collected
  them. A test that is never collected passes silently, which is the same shape as the defects this
  session has been finding.
- The MCP tool description for `memory_get_chunk` now states that decision ids resolve and that a
  decision payload carries `entry_context`, since neither was discoverable from the schema.
